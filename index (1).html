Cycle time defaults

Wait is time the robot stands while a person loads, unloads or confirms. Pick / drop is the robot's own handling, such as fork-in and lift on the pallet AMR. Extra delay covers doors, lifts and crossings. Efficiency scales top speed for slowdowns near people and at corners. Each route can override any of these.

Operation

Peak factor scales average hourly trips up to the busy hour. Use P90 ÷ average daily demand from your own data; 1.2–1.4 is common.

Manual baseline

Walking at 0.4 s/ft with no handling matches the simple method: trips × round-trip distance × pace × rate × days. Pace presets other than walking are typical figures: replace them with your own time studies. Handling stays with people under AMR too, so it changes the baseline but not the saving.

Routes and demand

Loads/day is the demand: requests, totes, carts or pallets. Trips = loads ÷ loads per trip, so batching cuts trips. Fill in the narrowest aisle on a route to check it against the robot. Routes with the same Group share a dedicated pool of robots.

How to use AMR Route Planner

AMR Route Planner turns a floor plan into an AMR fleet size and a business case. You trace routes on your layout, enter how much moves each day, and the app works out cycle times, robots needed and payback against moving loads by hand. It covers two robot types: the Pudu T300 for small parts (up to 300 kg) and the Pudu MP2000 for pallets (up to 2,000 kg).

Work through tabs 1 to 3 in order. The summary bar at the top updates as you change anything. The app opens on a demo site with example data so you can try every screen first.

Quick start: a new site in 10 minutes

  1. New project. Tap New project and name the site.
  2. Upload layout. Tap Upload layout and pick a PNG or JPG floor plan. Export a PDF page as an image first.
  3. Calibrate. Tap both ends of a dimension you know, such as a rack run or column spacing, and enter its real length.
  4. Add stations. Choose Add station and tap each pickup and drop point, then name it.
  5. Draw routes. Choose Draw route, tap the start station, tap each corner along the aisle, then tap the destination. Enter loads per day.
  6. Pick the AMR. On 2 · Demand & fleet, set AMR type to small parts (T300) or pallet (MP2000).
  7. Check demand and timings. Correct loads per day and loads per trip, then review the wait times.
  8. Set costs. On 3 · Business case, replace the default prices and installation costs with your quotes.
  9. Read the result. The summary bar shows robots needed and payback; the cash chart shows break-even.
  10. Save. Tap Save project for a backup file, or Export results CSV for a spreadsheet.

  • AMR type resets price, waits and handling to that robot's defaults. Reset to catalogue values undoes your edits.
  • Cycle time defaults: speed efficiency (0.75 typical, 0.6 in busy areas), wait at pickup and drop while people load, the robot's own pick and drop time, and extra delay for doors or lifts.
  • Operation: hours and days worked, peak-hour factor (about 1.3), target utilisation (0.75 leaves headroom), spare robots and robots per charger.
  • Manual baseline: how loads move today (walking, pallet truck or forklift sets the pace), labour rate and on-cost uplift. Handling time stays with people after automation.
  • Routes and demand: one row per route. Enter an override distance, loads per day, loads per trip (batching cuts trips), kg per trip and the narrowest aisle. Red chips flag over payload or too narrow an aisle. Routes with the same Group share robots.
  • Cycle time by route: override any timing for one route, cap speed in a slow zone, or enter a fixed cycle from a trial. Blank uses the default (grey); your values are outlined in orange.
  • Fleet by group: calculated robots per group, with charging and utilisation. Type a number in Your override to fix the fleet. Red means over 85% busy; amber under 25%.

3 · Business case

  • Buy (capex) or Robot-as-a-Service: RaaS swaps the robot price for a monthly fee.
  • Investment: robot and charger prices, integration, site prep, training, and top modules or carts per robot.
  • Running costs: maintenance as a yearly % of robot cost, and fleet software per robot per month.
  • People time kept per trip reduces the saving; equipment cost avoided adds retired forklifts or truck leases.
  • Savings basis: Hours released counts every hour; Whole FTEs only is the conservative view.
  • Outputs: investment, net saving per month, payback, NPV, ROI, the cash chart, cost build-up, and a sensitivity table at ±20% labour rate and 0.75× to 2× demand.

  • Save project writes a .amr.json file, including the layout, to Downloads/AMR Route Planner on Android.
  • Open file loads a saved project. Send the file to a colleague to share a design.
  • Export results CSV saves routes, trips, cycle times, robots and the financial summary for Excel.

How the numbers are calculated

  • Cycle = d ÷ (eff × loaded speed) + d ÷ (eff × empty speed) + pick + drop + waits + extra delay, where d is the one-way distance.
  • AMR trips/day = loads per day ÷ AMR loads per trip. Robot hours/day = trips × cycle.
  • Robots per group = peak-hour robot load ÷ (uptime × target utilisation), rounded up. Peak load = Σ trips × peak factor ÷ operating hours × cycle.
  • Charging: if each robot's daily work exceeds its run time, the fleet grows by charge time ÷ run time.
  • Manual hours/day = manual trips × (2d × pace + handling). Manual cost/month = hours × rate × (1 + uplift) × days.
  • Net/month = labour released + equipment avoided − maintenance − software (or RaaS fees). Payback = investment ÷ net/month.
